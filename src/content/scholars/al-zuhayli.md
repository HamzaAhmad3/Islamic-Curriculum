Wahbah ibn Mustafa al-Zuhayli was born in 1932 in Dayr 'Atiyah, a town on the road between Damascus and Homs. After ranking first in his class through Damascus's Sharia secondary school, he studied at al-Azhar's Faculty of Sharia (graduating top of his class, 1956), earned a law degree from Ain Shams University, and completed a doctorate in Islamic law at Cairo University in 1963 with highest honors, on the effects of war in Islamic jurisprudence across the eight madhhabs and international law. His teachers spanned both Damascus — including Hasan Habannakah al-Maydani in tafsir — and Cairo, including Shaykh al-Azhar Mahmud Shaltut and Muhammad Abu Zahrah.

He joined Damascus University in 1963, rising to full professor by 1975, and was seconded for extended periods to the University of Benghazi in Libya, the University of Khartoum, and — most consequentially — to UAE University in Al Ain for five years (1984–1989), during which he completed his major tafsir, **al-Tafsir al-Munir**. His output over a roughly fifty-year career was extraordinary: his eleven-volume **al-Fiqh al-Islami wa Adillatuh**, translated into Turkish, Malay, Persian, and Kurdish, became one of the most widely used comparative fiqh references in the modern Muslim world, and his shorter **al-Wajiz** volumes in fiqh, usul al-fiqh, and tafsir made the same material accessible at an introductory level — his **al-Tafsir al-Wajiz** is still commonly recommended as an early, accessible full read of the Qur'an. He served on Islamic Fiqh Academies in Jeddah, India, Sudan, and America, on Syria's Supreme Fatwa Council, and on Shari'ah supervisory boards for several Islamic banks. He died in Damascus on 9 August 2015 at the age of eighty-three.
