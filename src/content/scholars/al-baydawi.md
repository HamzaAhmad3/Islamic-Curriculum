Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Qasim Umar al-Baydawi, known as Nasir al-Din al-Baydawi, was a Shafi'i jurist, usuli, Ash'ari theologian, mufassir, muhaddith, grammarian, judge, and philosopher. He was born in al-Bayda in Fars near Shiraz, from which he took his nisbah, although his exact birth year is unknown and is generally placed in the early seventh century AH.

He studied under his father, the judge Abu al-Qasim Umar al-Baydawi, from whom he learned Shafi'i fiqh, as well as Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Kahta'i and Sharaf al-Din Umar al-Bushkani. He later became Qadi of Shiraz, where he taught and issued fatwas for many years before eventually leaving the judiciary and relocating to Tabriz, where he spent the remainder of his life.

Al-Baydawi was regarded as one of the foremost scholars of his era, combining expertise in fiqh, usul al-fiqh, tafsir, kalam, hadith, grammar, and philosophy. He was praised by later biographers as the leading scholar of Shiraz and Azerbaijan and was noted for combining the rational and transmitted sciences. Numerous students studied under him, including Ahmad al-Jarbardi, Umar al-Maraghi, Ali al-Khunji, and Taj al-Din al-Hindi.

His most famous work is Anwar al-Tanzil wa Asrar al-Ta'wil, one of the most influential classical Sunni tafsirs. Drawing upon al-Zamakhshari's al-Kashshaf, al-Razi's Mafatih al-Ghayb, and al-Raghib al-Isfahani's tafsir, it became one of the standard Qur'anic commentaries throughout the Muslim world and generated numerous commentaries and glosses. His other major works include Minhaj al-Wusul ila 'Ilm al-Usul, a foundational text in Shafi'i legal theory; Tawali' al-Anwar in theology; al-Ghayah al-Quswa fi Dirayat al-Fatwa in Shafi'i fiqh; Sharh al-Tanbih; Lubb al-Lubab fi 'Ilm al-I'rab; and Tuhfat al-Abrar fi Sharh Masabih al-Sunnah, among many others.

Al-Baydawi died in Tabriz in 685 AH (1286 CE), though some later reports mention 691 AH. He was buried beside Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi, in accordance with his request. His works, particularly Anwar al-Tanzil and Minhaj al-Wusul, remained central texts in the study of tafsir and usul al-fiqh for centuries and continue to be studied in traditional Islamic curricula.
