Abu 'Abd Allah Shams al-Din Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Ru'ayni, better known as al-Hattab, was born in Mecca on 18 Ramadan 902 AH. His family originated from Tripoli in North Africa, and his father had migrated to Mecca in 877 AH, where the family settled. Toward the end of his life, al-Hattab returned to Tripoli, where he died in 954 AH.

Al-Hattab studied under his father and other scholars of his time. He was a Maliki jurist and also devoted himself to tafsir, usul al-fiqh, Arabic grammar, and tasawwuf. His scholarship was firmly rooted in the Maliki tradition, and he became one of its most important later authorities.

His best-known work is **Mawahib al-Jalil fi Sharh Mukhtasar Khalil**, one of the most important and widely relied upon commentaries on Khalil ibn Ishaq's *Mukhtasar*. He also authored **Mutammimat al-Ajurrumiyyah**, a supplement to the *Ajurrumiyyah* that became a popular introductory text in Arabic grammar, and **Qurrat al-'Ayn fi Sharh al-Waraqat**, a commentary on Imam al-Juwayni's *al-Waraqat* in usul al-fiqh.

Al-Hattab died in Tripoli in 954 AH (1547 CE). A mosque known as Masjid al-Hattab was reportedly built there in his memory, and his works—particularly *Mawahib al-Jalil*—remain among the standard references studied in the Maliki school.
