Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Sawrah al-Sulami al-Tirmidhi, Abu Isa, was born in 209 AH in Tirmidh, on the Oxus. He studied extensively under Imam al-Bukhari and shared several of the same teachers, including Qutaybah ibn Sa'id and Ali ibn Hujr, and traveled widely across Khurasan, the Hejaz, and Iraq in pursuit of hadith. He went blind later in life and was renowned among his contemporaries for an exceptional memory, described as proverbial in his era.

His **Sunan** (al-Jami' al-Sahih) is one of the six canonically recognized hadith collections, and is distinguished among them, per later editor Ahmad Shakir's assessment, by three features rarely found together elsewhere: a notably concise treatment of transmission chains, systematic recording of where the jurists of different schools differed on a given legal question alongside the relevant hadith for each position, and detailed technical commentary (ta'lil) on the strength or weakness of individual hadith — making the work function as a practical manual of hadith methodology in its own right, not merely a collection. His **al-Shama'il al-Muhammadiyyah**, a dedicated compilation on the Prophet's appearance, character, and daily habits, remains the standard starting point for that specific genre of study. He died in Tirmidh in Rajab 279 AH, though al-Sam'ani records an alternative date of 275 AH in the nearby village of Bugh.
