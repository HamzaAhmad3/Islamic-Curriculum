Uthman ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Kurdi al-Shahrazuri, known as Ibn al-Salah, was born in 577 AH in Shahrazur (a village called Shirkhan nearby, in Kurdistan). He studied fiqh under his father before moving to Mosul, then undertook extensive travels for hadith across Baghdad (hearing from Ibn Sukaynah and Ibn Tabarzadh), Hamadhan, Nishapur, Marw (under Abu al-Muzaffar al-Sam'ani), Aleppo, Harran (under Abd al-Qadir al-Ruhawi), and Damascus (under Ibn Qudamah al-Maqdisi and Ibn Asakir). He taught for a time at the Salahiyyah madrasa in Jerusalem, then relocated to Damascus after the sultan ordered the demolition of Jerusalem's walls, eventually becoming shaykh of the newly founded Dar al-Hadith al-Ashrafiyyah and also teaching at al-Shamiyyah al-Sughra.

His **Muqaddimat Ibn al-Salah** (also known as Ma'rifat Anwa' 'Ulum al-Hadith) is the foundational systematic treatment of hadith terminology and methodology — nearly every later work in the discipline, including Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani's Nukhbat al-Fikr, engages directly with the categories Ibn al-Salah first laid out. Al-Dhahabi, who studied under him, described him as of firm religious commitment and traditionalist (salafi) orientation in creed, restrained from speculative theology. Ibn al-Salah issued a forceful fatwa against the study of logic and philosophy, calling it "the root of foolishness... a wellspring of deviation and heresy," and urged that its practitioners be removed from the madrasas — though he is also recorded as having studied some logic himself under Ibn Yunus al-Mawsili. He held one notably minority position, defending the validity of Salat al-Raghai'b despite acknowledging its hadith basis was weak, a stance that drew criticism including from Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam.

He died at dawn on Wednesday, 25 Rabi' al-Akhir 643 AH, in Damascus. Due to a Khwarizmian siege of the city at the time, only about ten men were able to carry his bier out to the Sufi cemetery for burial; his grave remained a visited site where supplication was said to be readily answered.
