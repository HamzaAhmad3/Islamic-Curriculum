Abu Muhammad al-Qasim ibn Firruh ibn Khalaf ibn Ahmad al-Ru'ayni al-Shatibi was born in 538 AH in Shatiba, a major city of eastern al-Andalus. He became blind at a young age, but his family cared for his education, and he memorized the Qur'an and studied hadith and fiqh before turning his attention toward the science of qira'at. He first studied in his hometown before traveling to Valencia, one of the major centers of learning in al-Andalus, where he studied under leading scholars and mastered the readings transmitted in the work of Abu Amr al-Dani, al-Taysir fi al-Qira'at al-Sab'.

Al-Shatibi later traveled for pilgrimage and eventually settled in Egypt in 572 AH. Upon entering Cairo, he was welcomed by the influential judge al-Qadi al-Fadil Abd al-Rahim ibn Ali al-Lakhmi, who recognized his scholarly rank, appointed him as the teacher of the Fadiliyyah School, and provided him a place to teach. His reputation spread widely, and students traveled from different regions to study Qur'anic readings with him. He completed his famous poem Hirz al-Amani wa Wajh al-Tahani in Egypt, along with other major works in Qur'anic sciences.

Al-Shatibi's greatest contribution was his poetic masterpiece Hirz al-Amani wa Wajh al-Tahani, commonly known as al-Shatibiyyah. The poem condensed Abu Amr al-Dani's al-Taysir fi al-Qira'at al-Sab', organizing the seven canonical Qur'anic readings into a memorable poetic form. The work became one of the most important texts in the study of qira'at, praised for its precision, literary excellence, and sophisticated use of symbolic notation for the names of readers and transmitters. Generations of scholars memorized, taught, and commented upon it, making it one of the most influential works in Qur'anic recitation studies.

In addition to al-Shatibiyyah, he authored Aqilat Atrab al-Qasa'id fi Asna al-Maqasid on Qur'anic script (rasm al-mushaf), and Nazimat al-Zuhar fi A'dad Ay al-Suwar on the numbering of Qur'anic verses. His works received extensive scholarly attention, with major commentaries written by scholars such as al-Sakhawi, Abu Shamah, al-Ja'bari, Ibn al-Qasih, and later authorities in the field of qira'at.

Contemporary scholars described al-Shatibi as an exceptional scholar with mastery of multiple disciplines. He was known for his knowledge of Qur'anic readings, hadith, Arabic language, and literature, as well as his devotion, humility, and discipline. He was reported to teach only while in a state of purity, avoiding unnecessary speech and dedicating himself to the Qur'an and knowledge.

Al-Shatibi died in Cairo on Sunday, 28 Jumada al-Akhirah 590 AH and was buried in al-Qarafah cemetery. His legacy remains firmly established in Islamic scholarship, particularly in the science of qira'at, where al-Shatibiyyah continues to serve as a central text for students and specialists throughout the Muslim world.
