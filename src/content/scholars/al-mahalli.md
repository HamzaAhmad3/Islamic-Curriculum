Jalal al-Din Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn Hashim al-Mahalli was a prominent Shafi'i jurist, usul scholar, and Qur'an exegete of the ninth century AH. He was born in Cairo at the beginning of Shawwal 791 AH (1389 CE) and was originally from al-Mahallah al-Kubra, from which his nisbah al-Mahalli is derived. He grew up in Cairo, memorized the Qur'an, studied foundational texts, and pursued a wide range of transmitted and rational sciences.

He studied fiqh, usul al-fiqh, and Arabic language under scholars including Shams al-Din al-Barmawi, Ibrahim al-Bayjuri, Jalal al-Din al-Bulqini, Abu Zur'ah al-'Iraqi, and Izz al-Din Ibn Jama'ah. He studied hadith sciences from al-Wali al-'Iraqi and Hafiz Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani, from whom he read the complete Sharh Alfiyyat al-'Iraqi and received permission to teach it. He also studied tafsir and usul al-din with Shams al-Din al-Bisati, and logic, rhetoric, disputation, and related sciences with Badr al-Din al-Aqsara'i. Through extensive study, he mastered both rational and transmitted disciplines and became one of the leading scholars of his generation.

Al-Mahalli was renowned for his intelligence, precision, and strength in scholarly debate. His contemporaries described him as exceptionally sharp-minded and deeply analytical, with a reputation for refusing error and carefully returning to the truth when it became clear. He was known for commanding respect among scholars and the public, for his ascetic lifestyle, and for his commitment to enjoining good and opposing wrongdoing. He was offered the position of chief judge but declined, stating his concern over its responsibility.

He taught at major institutions in Cairo, including the Burquqiyyah and Mu'ayyadiyyah madrasas, and became a sought-after authority for legal opinions. Students and scholars traveled to study with him, and many prominent scholars of the following generation emerged from his circle, including Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti, who completed his unfinished tafsir after his death. His writings were characterized by concise expression, careful analysis, and strong organization, causing them to become widely studied throughout the Islamic world.

His most famous contribution to Qur'anic studies was his tafsir, which he began and completed up to the end of Surah al-Isra' or al-Kahf according to different reports, leaving the work unfinished at his death. Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti later completed the remaining sections, producing Tafsir al-Jalalayn, which became one of the most widely read tafsirs in madrasas and scholarly circles.

In Shafi'i jurisprudence and legal theory, his works became foundational texts. Kanz al-Raghibin, his commentary on Imam al-Nawawi's Minhaj al-Talibin, became one of the most important explanations of the Minhaj and was widely taught with numerous later commentaries. His Al-Badr al-Tali' fi Hall Jam' al-Jawami', a commentary on Taj al-Din al-Subki's Jam' al-Jawami', became one of the major Shafi'i works in usul al-fiqh and received extensive attention from later scholars.

Al-Mahalli died in Cairo at the beginning of 864 AH (1459 CE) after suffering illness. He was buried near his ancestors after a large funeral attended by many people. Scholars mourned his death, and later biographers praised his knowledge, character, and lasting influence, stating that few scholars after him combined such breadth of knowledge and intellectual ability.
