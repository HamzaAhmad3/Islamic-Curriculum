Muhammad Ali ibn Jamil al-Sabuni was a prominent contemporary Syrian scholar, jurist, and Qur'an exegete. He was born in Aleppo in 1349 AH (1930 CE) into a scholarly family known for its connection to Islamic learning. His father, Shaykh Jamil al-Sabuni, was among the notable scholars of Aleppo, and from his family emerged several scholars. He received his earliest religious and Arabic education from his father and developed a strong attachment to Islamic sciences from a young age.

He memorized the Qur'an during his early education and studied Arabic, Islamic law, inheritance, and religious sciences. He initially enrolled in commercial studies in Aleppo but left due to his preference for Islamic studies and joined the Khasrawiyyah School (the Shari'ah College of Aleppo). There he studied tafsir, hadith, fiqh, usul al-fiqh, Arabic grammar, morphology, rhetoric, Islamic history, and other sciences under leading scholars of Aleppo.

After graduating from the Khasrawiyyah School in 1369 AH (1949 CE), he was sent by the Syrian Ministry of Endowments to study at al-Azhar University in Cairo. He graduated from the Faculty of Shari'ah in 1372 AH (1952 CE) and later obtained a specialized degree in Islamic judiciary in 1375 AH (1955 CE).

After completing his studies, al-Sabuni returned to Syria and taught Islamic studies in secondary schools in Aleppo until 1962 CE. He was then sent to Saudi Arabia as a professor at the Faculty of Shari'ah and Islamic Studies and the Faculty of Education at King Abdulaziz University in Makkah, where he taught for nearly three decades. He later worked as a researcher at the Center for Scientific Research and Revival of Islamic Heritage at Umm al-Qura University, where he contributed to the editing of classical works, including Ma'ani al-Qur'an by Abu Ja'far al-Nahhas. He also served as an advisor in the Scientific Miracles of the Qur'an and Sunnah department of the Muslim World League.

Al-Sabuni was especially known for his work in tafsir. His Safwat al-Tafasir, written in three volumes, became his most famous work and was widely distributed throughout the Muslim world. His Rawa'i al-Bayan fi Tafsir Ayat al-Ahkam also became a well-known modern reference on Qur'anic legal verses. His writings were translated into several languages and became widely used by students and readers of Islamic studies.

Alongside his academic work, al-Sabuni maintained a strong teaching and preaching presence. He delivered lessons at the Masjid al-Haram in Makkah, gave weekly tafsir lessons in Jeddah for several years, and recorded a complete Qur'an tafsir program consisting of hundreds of episodes for broadcast.

He participated in numerous Islamic conferences and scholarly gatherings. In 1431 AH (2010 CE), he was selected as the Islamic Personality of the Year by the Dubai International Holy Qur'an Award in recognition of his contributions to Qur'anic studies and Islamic scholarship.

Al-Sabuni was known for his dedication to teaching, writing, and research. His contemporaries described him as devoted to knowledge, humble despite his scholarly status, committed to worship and Qur'an recitation, and patient with criticism while remaining firm in his scholarly views.

He died on Friday 19 March 2021 CE (6 Sha'ban 1442 AH) in Yalova, Turkey, at the age of approximately ninety-one. He was buried in Istanbul near the grave of Necmettin Erbakan after a large funeral attended by scholars and students.
