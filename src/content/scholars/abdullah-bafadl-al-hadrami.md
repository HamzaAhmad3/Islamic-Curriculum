Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr Ba Fadl al-Hadrami al-Sa'di al-Madhhiji was a prominent Shafi'i jurist of Hadramawt, born in Tarim in 850 AH/1446 CE. He belonged to Banu Sa'd al-'Ashirah of Madhhij and became the foremost authority in Shafi'i jurisprudence in his region. He traveled in search of knowledge to al-Shihr, Aden, the Two Holy Sanctuaries, and other centers of learning before returning to Hadramawt, where he spent the remainder of his life teaching, issuing fatwas, and authoring works.

He studied under leading scholars of his time, including Abu al-Fath Sharaf al-Din Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Maraghi and Burhan al-Din ibn Zuhayr. Through persistent study and close companionship with his teachers, he gained recognition for his mastery of Shafi'i fiqh. His teachers praised both his scholarship and character, and he eventually became the principal jurist of Hadramawt, holding the chair of instruction at the congregational mosque of al-Shihr. He devoted his time to teaching, reading, worship, and legal consultation, and was widely respected by rulers and the general public alike. He also frequently acted as a mediator between the sultans and tribes of Hadramawt.

His most influential work is al-Muqaddimah al-Hadramiyyah, a concise primer in Shafi'i jurisprudence that became one of the standard introductory texts of the madhhab. Although it covers only the quarter of acts of worship, it achieved widespread acceptance among students. The work's importance is reflected in the fact that Ibn Hajar al-Haytami wrote his celebrated commentary al-Manhaj al-Qawim, while Muhammad al-Ramli authored a commentary on Ba Fadl's shorter legal epitome. Ba Fadl also wrote works on legal theory, devotional practice, astronomy, adhkar, and other subjects.

Contemporary and later biographers describe him as combining deep scholarship with piety, generosity, humility, and dedication to serving others. He was known for commanding good and forbidding wrong, assisting students, spending generously in charity, and preserving his time for teaching, study, worship, and remembrance of Allah. He remained the leading authority for legal instruction and fatwa in Hadramawt until his death in al-Shihr on 5 Ramadan 918 AH (1512 CE), after which he was buried there. His legal writings, especially al-Muqaddimah al-Hadramiyyah, continue to occupy an important place in the traditional Shafi'i curriculum.
