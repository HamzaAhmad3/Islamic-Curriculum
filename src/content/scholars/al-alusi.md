Mahmud ibn Abd Allah al-Husayni al-Alusi, known as Shihab al-Din Abu al-Thana', was a renowned Qur'anic exegete, hadith scholar, litterateur, and one of the leading scholars of Baghdad in the thirteenth century AH. Born in Baghdad in 1217 AH/1802 CE into the distinguished Alusi family, whose lineage traces through al-Husayn on his father's side and al-Hasan through Shaykh Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani on his mother's side, he devoted his life to the study and teaching of the Islamic sciences from an early age.

He studied under his father, Ali al-Suwaydi, Khalid al-Naqshbandi, Ali al-Mawsili, and other prominent scholars. He began teaching and writing while only thirteen years old, later serving as the Hanafi mufti of Baghdad after his appointment in 1248 AH. Following his dismissal from the office, he devoted himself entirely to scholarship. In 1262 AH he traveled through Mosul, Mardin, and Sivas to Istanbul, where Sultan Abd al-Majid honored him before he returned to Baghdad after an absence of twenty-one months.

Al-Alusi was recognized for his broad mastery of the Islamic sciences, possessing knowledge of the legal schools, comparative religion, literature, and theology. Although described by his son as Shafi'i by family tradition, he frequently followed the Hanafi school in legal practice and eventually inclined toward independent legal reasoning (ijtihad). His creed reflected the methodology of the Salaf, and contemporaries praised his commitment to the Sunnah and avoidance of sectarian disputes.

His greatest contribution is Ruh al-Ma'ani fi Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim wa al-Sab' al-Mathani, one of the most important tafsirs of the later Islamic tradition. The work synthesizes earlier exegetical scholarship while incorporating discussions of hadith, language, jurisprudence, theology, rhetoric, and Qur'anic sciences. In addition to his tafsir, he authored works on travel, literature, grammar, logic, theology, and legal responsa, including Nashwat al-Shumul fi al-Safar ila Istanbul, Nuzhat al-Albab fi Ghara'ib al-Ightirab, al-Ajwibah al-'Iraqiyyah, Sharh al-Sullam, and Hashiyah 'ala Sharh al-Qatr.

Al-Alusi died in Baghdad on 21 Dhu al-Qa'dah 1270 AH (1854 CE) and was buried near the grave of Ma'ruf al-Karkhi. He is remembered as one of the last great classical Qur'anic exegetes, whose Ruh al-Ma'ani remains a standard reference in tafsir and continues to be widely studied throughout the Islamic world.
