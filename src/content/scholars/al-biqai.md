Burhan al-Din Ibrahim ibn Umar al-Biqa'i was born around 809 AH in Khirbat Ruha in the Biqa' region of present-day Lebanon. He later moved to Damascus with his maternal grandfather before continuing his studies in Jerusalem and eventually Cairo, where he received advanced instruction in the Qur'anic sciences, fiqh, Arabic grammar, hadith, and other Islamic disciplines. During his travels he also studied in Aleppo, Alexandria, Damietta, the Hijaz, and remained for periods in Mecca, Medina, and Ta'if.

Al-Biqa'i studied under many of the leading scholars of his generation, most notably Ibn al-Jazari in the Ten Qira'at, Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani in hadith, and Taqi al-Din Ibn Qadi Shuhbah in Shafi'i fiqh. His scholarly abilities earned widespread recognition, and he later taught hadith and qira'at in Cairo, where large numbers of students studied under him before he eventually returned to Damascus. Among his most famous students was Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti.

A prolific author, al-Biqa'i wrote works across numerous disciplines including tafsir, qira'at, tajwid, fiqh, history, biography, Arabic language, and theology. His most celebrated work, **Nazm al-Durar fi Tanasub al-Ayat wa al-Suwar**, was one of the earliest comprehensive tafsirs devoted to demonstrating the thematic coherence and structural relationship between Qur'anic verses and surahs. Later scholars, including al-Shawkani, praised it as an exceptionally original contribution to Qur'anic exegesis and frequently consulted it when other tafsirs did not resolve difficult interpretive questions.

Late in his career, al-Biqa'i became the subject of significant controversy after citing material from the Torah and Gospel within his tafsir to illustrate Qur'anic passages. Some contemporaries strongly objected and even sought legal action against him, while al-Biqa'i defended the permissibility of such citations through a dedicated treatise. Later historians, including al-Shawkani, regarded much of the criticism directed toward him as influenced by scholarly rivalry rather than objective assessment, particularly in light of documented disputes between al-Biqa'i and al-Sakhawi.

Al-Biqa'i spent the final years of his life in Damascus, continuing to write and teach despite persistent opposition. He died on the night of Saturday, 18 Rajab 885 AH and was buried outside Damascus near the grave of 'Atikah. He remains remembered as one of the foremost Shafi'i scholars of the ninth century AH and as a pioneering exegete whose work on the coherence of the Qur'an continues to attract scholarly attention.
