Ahmad ibn al-Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Isfahani, known as al-Qadi Abu Shuja', was a distinguished Shafi'i jurist and judge. His grandfather was from Isfahan and his father was born in Abadan, while Abu Shuja' himself was born in Basra in 434 AH. He taught the Shafi'i madhhab in Basra for more than forty years and became widely renowned for his knowledge, piety, and integrity. He was appointed as a judge in 447 AH, earning a reputation as a just judge who did not fear criticism in upholding the truth.

Later in life he settled in Medina, where he devoted himself to serving the Prophet's Mosque until his death in 593 AH. Biographical works describe him as having lived an exceptionally long life while retaining his physical faculties. It is related that when asked about this, he replied, "I did not use any of my limbs in disobedience to Allah in my youth, so Allah preserved them for me in old age."

Abu Shuja' is best known for authoring Ghayat al-Ikhtisar, also known as al-Ghayah wa al-Taqrib or Matn Abi Shuja', which he composed at the request of some of his companions as a concise introduction to Shafi'i jurisprudence. He intended it to be easy for beginners to study and memorize, organizing it with numerous classifications and concise summaries. The work became one of the foundational texts of the Shafi'i madhhab and has been studied continuously for centuries. He also authored a commentary on al-Mawardi's al-Iqna'.
